## Overview

CitiFlowAI was built for the **Huawei Developer Competition 2025 (Asia Pacific)**. We
reached the APAC Grand Final in Hong Kong, pitching at PolyU as the **only team representing
Indonesia** in our track.

## The problem

CitiFlowAI is a **Traffic Management as a Service (TMaaS)** platform built on Huawei Cloud.
We anchored it to a problem we live with: in the Greater Jakarta area, traffic congestion
costs an estimated **Rp 100 trillion (about $6.1 billion) a year** in wasted fuel, lost
productivity and health costs, and the average Jakarta driver loses **89 hours a year** stuck
in traffic. Building more roads can't keep up, so the realistic fix is to make the existing
roads work better.

Most "smart" traffic systems still only react: lights run on timers, or a central model looks
at past trends and suggests changes for engineers to approve. CitiFlowAI tries to act before
gridlock forms. Each intersection is an AI agent, and the agents work together to create
"green wave" corridors and clear the way for emergency vehicles.

## What I did

I led the team and owned the machine-learning work, including framing the problem as multi-agent RL, 
building the SUMO simulation and the ModelArts training pipeline, and coordinating the team into the
APAC final. I also drove the pitch during the pitching event.

## Building it

Fixed-cycle lights can't react to live traffic, and a controller that only looks at one
intersection often just pushes the jam to the next one. So I treated each intersection as an
agent in a shared environment and trained them together with **Multi-Agent Reinforcement
Learning (MARL)**, so they learn to cooperate instead of each being greedy. The simulations ran
in **SUMO** and training ran on **Huawei ModelArts**.

The system is split between the **edge and the cloud**, because speed matters most here. Sending
camera video to the cloud and back takes 500–1000 ms, and by then an ambulance is already in the
intersection. So:

- **Edge:** a **Huawei Atlas 500** box at each intersection runs the trained policy on the live
  video and makes decisions in under 200 ms.
- **Cloud:** ModelArts collects anonymized data from every intersection, learns city-wide
  patterns, and trains the main MARL model that coordinates green waves along whole corridors.

Video is processed at the intersection and never stored; only anonymized data is sent to the
cloud, where it feeds live dashboards and long-term reports.

The model also keeps improving. Every night ModelArts retrains on that day's data, and the new
model is rolled out to each Atlas box with a **blue-green deployment**: the new version starts
next to the old one, gets checked, and then takes over, so there's no downtime.

## Outcome

In simulation, the MARL agents moved **6× more vehicles** than a fixed-cycle baseline, and the
edge-cloud setup ran **about 73% faster** than a cloud-only one, which is what makes real-time
green waves and emergency priority possible.

We also made the business case. Sold as a subscription, TMaaS turns a large upfront
infrastructure cost into a predictable monthly one, which makes it easier for cities with tight
budgets to adopt. We closed the pitch with the live demo.
