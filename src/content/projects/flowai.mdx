## The brief

CitiFlowAI was built for the **Huawei Developer Competition 2025 (Asia Pacific)** — a
build-and-pitch contest judged across four weighted dimensions: technical architecture
(30%), creativity and innovation (30%), functionality and impact (20%), and business
value (20%). We were one of the team that went to the APAC Grand Final in
Hong Kong, where we pitched at PolyU as the **only team representing Indonesia** in our track.

## The idea

CitiFlowAI is an intelligent **Traffic Management as a Service (TMaaS)** platform built on
Huawei Cloud. We anchored it to a problem we live with: in the Greater Jakarta area,
traffic congestion drains an estimated **Rp 100 trillion (≈ $6.1 billion) a year** in wasted
fuel, lost productivity, and health costs, and the average Jakarta driver loses **89 hours
a year** stuck in traffic — a 37% jump from 2023. Road networks grow at roughly 0.3% a year,
so you can't build your way out of it; the only scalable fix is to make the existing grid
dramatically more efficient.

Most "smart" traffic systems are still fundamentally reactive — timer-based controllers,
or centralized cloud models that *adapt to* past trends and hand engineers recommendations
to approve. CitiFlowAI flips that to *proactive*: each intersection becomes an AI agent
that predicts and prevents gridlock before it forms, and the agents cooperate to roll
"Green Wave" corridors and clear paths for emergency vehicles. Delivered as a
subscription, TMaaS turns a massive upfront CAPEX project into a predictable OPEX line —
which is what actually unblocks adoption for cash-constrained cities.

## My role

I led the team and owned the machine-learning work, including framing the problem as multi-agent RL, 
building the SUMO simulation and the ModelArts training pipeline, and coordinating the team into the
APAC final. I also drove the pitch during the pitching event.

## Building it

Fixed-cycle traffic lights can't react to live demand, and single-agent controllers
optimize one intersection while pushing congestion onto their neighbours. Coordinating
many intersections at once is a genuinely multi-agent problem: each signal's best action
depends on what every adjacent signal is doing. I framed each intersection as an agent in a
shared environment and trained them with **Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL)** so
they learn cooperative policies instead of locally-greedy ones, with simulations running in
**SUMO** and training on **Huawei ModelArts**.

The system runs on a **hybrid edge-cloud architecture**, because the hard constraint here is
latency. A pure-cloud loop — camera → cloud → traffic controller — runs 500–1000 ms
round-trips; by the time a centralized model spots an ambulance, the ambulance is already in
the intersection. So inference lives at the edge:

- **Edge (micro-intelligence):** a **Huawei Atlas 500** AI edge server at each intersection
  runs the trained policy on live video locally, making sub-200 ms decisions. It handles
  16-channel HD video and is built fan-free for −40 °C to +70 °C — it mounts on a traffic
  pole, not in a data centre.
- **Cloud (macro-intelligence):** **ModelArts** ingests anonymized metadata from every node,
  learns city-wide patterns, and trains the master MARL model that coordinates Green Waves
  across whole corridors.

The data plane keeps the two honest: edge nodes stream anonymized metadata through **DIS**,
which fans out to **Cloud Stream** for real-time dashboard metrics and dumps to **OBS** as a
historical data lake; **MRS** batch-processes OBS for long-term trend reports, and **IoTDA**
manages device authentication and reports node health. Privacy is handled at the edge —
video is processed locally and never stored; only aggregated, anonymized metadata leaves
over an encrypted, IoTDA-authenticated connection.

The piece I'm proudest of is the **CI/CD/CT (Continuous Integration / Deployment / Training)**
loop. ModelArts retrains nightly on the day's fresh data; the new model is pushed by **IEF
(Intelligent EdgeFabric)**, which runs a **Blue-Green deployment** on each Atlas box — start
the new container alongside the old, validate it, swap the connection instantly, retire the
old one. The grid gets measurably smarter every 24 hours with zero downtime.

## Pitch day

The judging panel skewed heavily business — finance, management, smart-city development —
so we balanced the technical depth against a hard commercial case: TMaaS shifts cities from
CAPEX to OPEX, we projected ~73% gross margin on a standard pilot, and we sized the
Indonesian ITS market at USD 6.8B (2025) growing to USD 18.7B by 2031 (17.9% CAGR), inside an
APAC smart-cities market that's the largest in the world. We closed on the demo.

## Outcome & takeaways

In simulation, the MARL agents processed **6× more vehicles** than a traditional
fixed-cycle baseline, and the edge-cloud architecture ran **~73% faster** than a cloud-only
setup — the latency headroom that makes real-time Green Waves and emergency prioritization
possible. 

It is unfortunate that we were unable to make it to the podium, other teams such as the startup teams already had real-world implementations 
and better business models, while others had real use-cases. I feel that's where we fall short, as we were still students without any real 
implementation or use-case, and the product we showcased was still ultimately a simulation.

This event has given me a big wake-up call, especially in handling a team and performing in a competition. I feel that I still had so much
to learn, and this experience has taught me a lot. Even so, I am truly grateful to be able to be in the grand finalist position in my
first ever competition.
